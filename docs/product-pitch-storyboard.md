# Lead Qualification & Routing Agent

## Client pitch storyboard

This storyboard is designed for a one-page infographic, a 75–90 second motion video, or the opening sequence of a sales deck.

The central message is:

> Every inquiry becomes a qualified, routed, and actionable opportunity—without a team manually checking multiple tools.

## Product story in one sentence

The platform collects leads from websites, email, and connected CRMs, enriches and scores them with AI, routes them to the right workspace or agent, alerts the right channel, and keeps the CRM synchronized.

## Visual direction

- **Tone:** confident, intelligent, operational, trustworthy.
- **Style:** dark graphite background, warm white type, electric orange for actions, cyan for data flow, green for successful outcomes.
- **Motif:** one lead signal travels through a glowing pipeline and becomes a closed-loop customer workflow.
- **Animation language:** incoming pulses, clean cards, expanding nodes, route lines, progress rings, subtle dashboard movement.
- **Avoid:** fake customer logos, unreadable UI text, exaggerated “fully autonomous” claims, API keys, or private customer data.
- **Recommended formats:** 16:9 for a pitch video, 1:1 or 4:5 for social, 1920×1080 for the hero infographic.

## The end-to-end flow

Capture → Normalize → Analyze → Score → Route → Notify → Sync → Follow up → Improve

## Infographic layout

Use a horizontal five-panel composition with a circular feedback loop underneath.

### Panel 1 — Capture

Show four input icons feeding a single inbox:

- Website form
- Gmail / Google
- Outlook
- Salesforce / HubSpot

Caption: **Bring every inquiry into one workflow.**

### Panel 2 — Understand

Show a lead card with four analysis chips:

- Fit
- Intent
- Urgency
- Temperature: Hot / Warm / Cold

Add a score ring and a small “Why this score?” explanation panel.

Caption: **Make lead quality visible and explainable.**

### Panel 3 — Route

Show a decision node branching to three workspaces or teams. Put small capacity bars beside each destination. Use weighted arrows to communicate admin-defined routing.

Caption: **Send the lead to the best available team.**

### Panel 4 — Activate

Show a Slack notification, a CRM update, and a follow-up email connected to the same lead ID.

Caption: **Notify, assign, and follow up without losing context.**

### Panel 5 — Improve

Show an admin control panel with routing ratios, workspace performance, lead volume, and conversion feedback.

Caption: **Use feedback to improve the system over time.**

### Feedback loop

Connect the final panel back to the qualification panel with:

> Every outcome makes the next routing decision clearer.

## Admin and team workflow inset

Add a small secondary flow below the main pipeline:

1. Admin creates a workspace.
2. Admin generates a staff access code.
3. Staff joins the correct workspace.
4. Admin sets routing weights, rules, and Slack destinations.
5. The system distributes new leads and reports load.

Callout: **Central control for admins. Clear access for teams.**

### Recommended live-screen sequence

1. Start on the real lead dashboard.
2. Click a lead and open the detail card in place.
3. Scroll or tab through its score, reason, routing, sync, and follow-up states.

5. Open routing settings and briefly show the selected workspace and distribution weight.
6. Open integrations and show healthy connection badges.
7. Finish on the activity timeline with the completed workflow.

### How to stage the demo safely

- Create a dedicated demo workspace and demo users.
- Use fictional names, domains, phone numbers, and email addresses.
- Seed three visually different leads: one hot, one warm, and one cold.
- Create at least two workspaces with different routing weights and visible capacity.
- Use a dedicated Slack demo channel and a sandbox CRM account.
- Use a non-production email mailbox for the confirmation-email scene.
- Pre-connect integrations before recording so no consent screen or secret appears in the final edit.
- Verify the dashboard has no browser notifications, account avatars, real customer data, API keys, tokens, or internal URLs.

### Motion treatment for real dashboard footage

Keep the real UI sharp and use motion graphics around it:

- Add a soft orange outline around the lead currently being discussed.
- Use a cyan line to connect the dashboard shot to the matching workflow label.
- Zoom 105–115% into the important card instead of cropping away the product identity.
- Freeze-frame the score, temperature, and next-step sections for 1–2 seconds.
- Use a small caption such as **Captured**, **Analyzed**, **Routed**, **Synced**, or **Follow-up ready**.
- Place generated abstract transitions between real UI shots; do not fake product interactions inside generated footage.
- Add a subtle cursor highlight and click ripple so viewers can follow the action.
